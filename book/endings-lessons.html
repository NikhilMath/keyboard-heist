Keyboard Heist

What the endings teach

One lesson for every ending. Read it once you’ve found yours.

The endings

The plateau. Chasing an object to feel alive leaves you right where you started.

The one who didn’t laugh. Connection fixes what stuff can’t. Say the true thing to someone who will listen.

Eleven years. A life without effort isn’t happiness. Numbness can pass for contentment.

The first no. What you refuse defines you as much as what you take.

Not the hero. Owning up is right, but it doesn’t erase what you did.

The lower plateau. You take your emptiness with you, even to Hell.

Driver unavailable. Every shortcut has a price, and it comes with strings.

Lost and found. Wanting is often just not having. Taken things don’t fill you.

Your own keyboard. Meaning comes from making things, not taking them.

A little spice. The people you share the ride with are the spice.

The far side. Doing right, and doing what you’re good at, makes everything especially interesting.

The dead ends

Asset not found. Cut corners get noticed, and sometimes all you had to do was ask.

Method. A lie grows past your control, and you end up owing the liar.

The fall guy. Once you’re part of something wrong, exposing it exposes you too.

The endings about getting the thing leave you on the plateau. The ones that escape it are about people, or about making something of your own.
